Practice

Tools that I use as a working musician to improve my musicianship.

Vocabulary · improvising

Phrasebook

Collect the licks and phrases you love in staff notation, add the chords, then drill them through all twelve keys and every mode. Theory is the grammar — this is what you actually say.

Ear training · harmony

Chorale Ear Trainer

Hear a few bars of a Bach chorale, then name the chords you hear in Roman numerals. Real four-part harmony, real Bach — the same chorales students have sharpened their ears on for 300 years. The harder cousin of the arcade chord quiz.

Train your ear
Ear training · pitch

Pitch Practice

Pure note ear training — no game. Hear a short sequence off a reference tonic and name what you heard. Pick the length, choose pentatonic up to fully chromatic, and stay in C or move through every key.

Train your ear
Play by ear · reading 🎤 listens to your playing

Melody Trainer

Read a tune on the stave and play or sing it back — your mic listens, and the cursor only advances when you land the right pitch. Ukulele, guitar, banjo, piano or voice — and it comes loaded with 200+ real tunes: jigs, reels, fiddle tunes and trad jazz.

Chords · rhythm · theory

Song Trainer

Learn a song by its chords and strumming, play along in time, then dig into the key, the scales you can solo with and the theory behind it. Chords & theory only — no lyrics.

Backing track · jam

Jam Track

Write a chord chart like a real lead sheet — drop chords over the bars, pick a style and a tempo, and the band plays it back on a loop. A band that never gets tired of backing you — solo over it, jam over it, train your ear on the changes.

Improvisation 🎤 listens to your playing

The Listening Lab

Solo over jazz and blues charts in any key — and the lab listens. Set your own rules (pentatonic, blue notes, chord tones, passing tones), then see your solo written out and graded, note by note.
