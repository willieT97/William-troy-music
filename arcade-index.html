Tuner

A chromatic tuner for any instrument — play a note and the needle shows how sharp or flat you are, with the nearest pitch named. Your browser will ask to use the microphone — that’s it listening. Handy before any of the games below.

Uke Progress

A run of ukulele chords plays, played with the same shapes you're learning — the last card is blank, so name the chord you hear. Starts with C, G, Am and F and adds one more each level up to the sevenths and Gm.

Triads

Triad Drop

You're handed a triad grip and a chord name — slide it up or down the neck to the one fret where the shape spells that chord. Majors, minors and all three inversions.

Triads

Triad Launch

Same puzzle, launched. Charge a power meter and fire the grip up the neck — it slides, bounces and settles. Land it on the fret that spells the chord: 3 points spot-on, 1 within a fret.

Piano

One octave — build the chord
Arcade

Chord Catapult

Fling a note in an arc onto the keyboard and strike the keys that spell the chord — C, E, G. Fifteen levels climb from triads through inversions, 7ths, 9ths and diminished chords, with trees, clouds and crosswind in the way. By the time you beat the wind, spelling chords is automatic.

Play
Falling

Chord Cascade

A chord's key-shape drops from the sky — scroll the piano roll so it lands on the keys that actually spell it. Since every chord's black-and-white pattern is unique, it only clicks in one place. Ramps through minors, inversions, 7ths and 9ths — chord spellings stick before you notice you've learned them.

Pitch Invaders

Aliens fall down the lane of their note — tap the matching note to shoot them down. Climb the levels and they cloak, so you go by ear alone. Boss waves play a run of notes to echo back.

Chromatic

Pitch Invaders — Chromatic

The full twelve notes on a piano keyboard, sharps and flats included. Start on the white keys, then a new accidental joins every few levels so your ear can settle before the next. Cloaked aliens push you to go by ear.

Circle of fifths

Pitch Invaders — Circle of Fifths

Only the seven notes of the key fall as aliens, and those piano keys glow to guide you. Clear a boss wave and the wheel spins to a new key — a quick home-note cue re-anchors your ear before the aliens return.

Progressions

Chord Progress

A chord progression plays with a marker sweeping across it — the last card stays blank. Name the chord you heard from the key's chords. Later levels add more chords, longer progressions, and inversions to spot.

Theory

Read it — play it
Rhythm

Rhythm Runner

Read the bar of rhythm, then the road opens into holes shaped like the notes. Hold to stay airborne for long notes, land on the rests — don't fall in or hit the ceiling. Beamed quavers, dotted notes and syncopation as the tempo climbs — keep running and you're reading rhythm at sight, no counting on fingers.

Play
Note values

Bar Packer

Pack a suitcase-shaped bar with the right note blocks so it fills exactly — no gaps, no overflow. A limited hand with a spare or two makes you work out which notes add up. Fill it and hear the rhythm play back. Meters vary from 4/4 to 6/8 and 7/8.

Play
Note values

The Missing Piece

A bar drawn as a jigsaw with one piece missing. Pick the note-value piece that fills the hole exactly — the surgical "what plus an eighth makes a beat?" drill. It snaps in and plays back. Meters and note values widen as you climb.

Play
Equivalence

Fix the Plank

A bar of note-planks with one cracked. Pull the broken plank out, then rebuild its length from smaller planks — the exact note isn't in your pile, so a half note becomes two crotchets. Fill the gap and the bar plays whole again.

Play
Reading

Note Reading

A note appears on the stave — name it before the clock runs down. Trains your reading across the lines and spaces, from the easy middle notes out to the ledger lines.

New here? Welcome.

The Music Arcade is a pile of free browser games and practice tools that teach real music — ears, reading, rhythm, songwriting — built by a music teacher in Ireland. No downloads, no sign-up needed.

Three questions, and we'll pick your first three things ▸

Everything here runs free in the browser — no ads and nothing to install. A few tools listen through the microphone to hear your child play; they're marked with a 🎤 badge, and the browser always asks permission first.

An account is optional — it saves songs and course progress. The three Dojo courses are free to start, with the later modules part of a small Pro subscription. If your child's teacher uses this site, their lesson emails may link straight to pages here to practise between lessons.

There's a private Rollbook for teachers: a weekly timetable with lesson notes, and one-click parent emails that link to the exact pages you used in the lesson.

Create a free account and choose “I teach” — the Rollbook tab appears in the site menu.
