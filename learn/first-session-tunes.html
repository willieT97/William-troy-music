10 first session tunes for beginners

From William · tenor banjo and Irish trad teacher

These are the ten tunes I give new students when they want to start playing at sessions. They’re all on the notes of D or G major, they sit in first position on banjo, mandolin or fiddle, and they build up in groups: three slow tunes, three polkas, three hornpipes and barndances, then your first jig. Learn them in this order and you’ll have a set ready for most sessions.

  1. The Dawning Of The Day (Fáinne Geal An Lae)

    air · D major

    A beautiful slow air in D, and the tune of Raglan Road. Its long notes give you time to make every one ring.

  2. Sí Bheag Sí Mhór

    waltz · D major

    A slow waltz in D, with room to make every note ring. Said to be the first tune O’Carolan wrote.

    ▶ Play along in the Melody TrainerSheet music & tab

  3. Fanny Power

    air · G major

    Another O’Carolan tune, in G. It moves at an easy pace, so you can put all your attention into a clean, even tone.

    ▶ Play along in the Melody TrainerSheet music & tab

  4. The Britches Full Of Stitches

    polka · D major

    Short and bouncy, and it sits right inside the D major scale. The classic first polka.

    ▶ Play along in the Melody TrainerSheet music & tab

  5. Egan's (The Kerry Polka)

    polka · D major

    The easiest tune on the list: two short parts that keep coming back to the same few notes. Play it straight after the Britches and you have your first set.

  6. John Ryan's

    polka · D major

    The same feel as the first two polkas, a little wider. Add it to the Britches and the Kerry and you have a full set of three polkas.

  7. The Boys Of Bluehill

    hornpipe · D major

    Your first hornpipe, in D. Play the pairs of notes long-short and it starts to bounce.

  8. The Stack Of Barley

    barndance · G major

    A barndance in G, with the same swung feel as a hornpipe. Easy-going and fun to play.

  9. Off To California

    hornpipe · G major

    A second hornpipe, in G. Give it the same swing as the Bluehill and the two make a pair.

  10. The Kesh

    jig · G major

    Your jig, and a session staple. Jigs go in threes; once this one flows, the jigs in the tune directory are open to you.

How I’d learn them

  • One tune at a time. Open it in the Melody Trainer: it shows every note on your instrument and waits for you to play it, so you can go as slowly as you need.
  • Then learn it by ear. Trad is passed on by ear, so once you know a tune, practise hearing a phrase and playing it back with Play It Back.
  • Put them into sets. The three polkas go together, and the Bluehill and Off to California make a pair of hornpipes. Playing tunes back to back is what a session sounds like.
  • Speed comes last. Play it clean and steady with the metronome first, then nudge the tempo up.

New to the banjo altogether? Start with my Beginner Banjo course: it gets you tuned up and playing the D major scale before the first of these tunes.

Why these ten

They’re all on the notes of D or G major, and those two key signatures cover nearly every tune you’ll hear at a session. They mix slow tunes, polkas, hornpipes, a barndance and a jig, so you’re ready for whatever gets started. And they’re well known, so you’ll hear them, and people will join in.
