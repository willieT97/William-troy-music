What key are Irish session tunes in?

Mostly D major and G major. Of the 103 popular session tunes I’ve gathered here, 96 use the key signature of D or G major, one or two sharps. About a quarter are in E or A dorian, which use those same notes. That’s why I start every new banjo student on the D scale, then G: between them, they cover nearly every tune at a session.

Same notes, different home

Only 61 of the 103 tunes (59%) are in a plain major key, so you might expect to need a lot of scales. You don’t. E dorian uses exactly the notes of D major; A dorian and D mixolydian use the notes of G major. The tune just treats a different note as home, which is what gives so many reels that lonesome, minor-ish sound.

Group the tunes by key signature instead and it’s almost all one or two sharps:

  • G major51 (50%)
  • D major45 (44%)
  • A major3 (3%)
  • C major3 (3%)
  • F major1 (1%)

So when a student asks me what to learn after D and G, my answer is: nothing new yet. Play the same notes, and listen for where the tune comes to rest.

What this means if you’re learning

  1. Learn D major first, then G major. That’s the order in my Beginner Banjo course, and it’s why: those two key signatures cover 96 of these 103 tunes. Start with D major on tenor banjo, then G major.
  2. Then learn to hear the dorian tunes. 24 of the 103 are in E or A dorian. You already know the notes; try Cooley's and listen for the E it keeps coming home to.
  3. Stay in first position. 102 of the 103 tunes fit between the open G string and the fourth finger on the E string, so there’s no need to move up the neck to play the session repertoire.

Across the Atlantic, and beyond

The same count tells other traditions apart. In the 50 American old-time fiddle tunes here, A major is the second most common key (14 tunes, 28%), and only 4 aren’t in a plain major key. In the 50 trad jazz tunes, the keys swing to the flat side: 34 of 50 (68%) are in B♭, E♭ or F, the keys of the horns.

Questions people ask

What is the most common key in Irish traditional music?

D major, then G major. In this collection 30 of 103 tunes are in D major and 27 in G major, and 96 of the 103 use the key signature of one of those two.

What does dorian mean in an Irish tune?

A dorian tune uses the notes of a major scale but treats a different note as home. E dorian uses the notes of D major, and A dorian uses the notes of G major, so they sound minor without needing any new notes.

Which scales should I learn first for Irish tunes?

D major, then G major. Those two key signatures cover 96 of the 103 tunes here, including the dorian and mixolydian ones, because those use the same notes.

How I counted

The Irish tunes are thesession.org’s most-saved tunes, plus a hand-picked set of well-known marches, barndances and slow airs: 103 in all, each using the first setting on thesession.org. The American fiddle tunes come from Jim’s Roots & Blues and the trad jazz tunes from The Public Domain Song Anthology.

Each key is the one written in that transcription. Transcribers don’t always agree, so the same tune can be written as E minor in one place and E dorian in another. “First position” means every note lies between the open G string of a fiddle (the G below middle C) and the B played with the fourth finger on the E string, the same written range on mandolin and tenor banjo. These are popular tunes, not every tune ever played, so treat the numbers as a picture of what you’ll hear at a typical session.
